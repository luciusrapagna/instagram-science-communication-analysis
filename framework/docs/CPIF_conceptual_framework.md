# CPIF conceptual framework

## Formal definition

The Communication Performance Interpretation Framework (CPIF) is a proposed, platform-independent conceptual and analytical framework for the context-sensitive interpretation of quantitative metrics recorded by digital platforms in university science-communication initiatives. CPIF structures the analytical pathway from communication context and metric definition to preparation, statistical integration, interpretation, and decision support. It does not generate a universal score and does not treat platform metrics as direct measures of educational or societal impact.

## Scientific rationale and methodological problem

Platform dashboards make communication traces readily available, but availability does not establish construct validity. Counts such as views, reach, likes, shares, or clicks may refer to different units, accumulation rules, time windows, and audience processes. Reporting these values in isolation can collapse visibility, response, audience growth, and content consumption into an undifferentiated notion of engagement. It may also obscure denominator validity, cumulative exposure time, distributional features, dependence among observations, multiplicity, uncertainty, and the institutional or editorial conditions under which the data were produced.

CPIF addresses this problem by requiring explicit separation of constructs and an auditable sequence of methodological decisions. It extends interpretation beyond descriptive reporting while remaining flexible about statistical methods. The appropriate analysis depends on the study design, data distribution, sampling structure, number of groups, dependence between observations, and research questions.

## Conceptual foundations

CPIF rests on five commitments: performance is multidimensional; platform metrics represent distinct processes; statistical evidence should include magnitude and uncertainty; performance is context-dependent; and platform traces are proxies rather than direct measures of effectiveness. The framework treats platforms as measurement environments whose definitions and interfaces may change. Consequently, metric provenance and extraction date are part of the evidence, not administrative details.

### Communication exposure

Communication exposure concerns the distribution and visibility of content. Potential indicators include views, reach, impressions, and video plays. These indicators are not interchangeable: a view can be an event, an impression can repeat for one account, and reach may represent estimated unique accounts. Operational definitions must be recorded for the relevant platform and extraction date.

### Communication interaction

Communication interaction concerns observable platform-recorded actions associated with content exposure. Potential indicators include likes, comments, shares, and saves. Follows and clicks may instead have a primary role in audience growth or navigation behaviour, depending on the platform definition, attribution window, and research question. Different actions may require different levels of effort and may express different audience responses; CPIF therefore does not combine them by default into a value-weighted hierarchy. A total interaction count may be reported when its components and construction are transparent, but it does not become a global performance score.

### Communication effectiveness

Communication effectiveness concerns outcomes such as learning, knowledge acquisition, scientific literacy, trust in science, behavioural change, community participation, dialogue, or societal impact. Platform-recorded exposure and interaction may motivate hypotheses about effectiveness, but they do not directly establish it. Effectiveness requires measurements appropriate to the outcome, such as validated surveys, knowledge assessments, interviews, observation, experimental designs, longitudinal follow-up, or community-level indicators.

## Six-layer architecture

1. **Communication Context** specifies the initiative, objectives, intended audience, institution, platform, period, editorial calendar, paid or organic promotion, and other conditions relevant to interpretation.
2. **Platform-Recorded Metrics** documents metric definitions and assigns each indicator a justified primary analytical role, with secondary roles where necessary, among exposure, interaction, audience growth, content consumption, navigation or click behaviour, or other platform-specific groups.
3. **Metric Preparation and Standardization** covers data cleaning, missingness, operational definitions, format classification, comparability, valid normalization, cumulative exposure time, and analytical outcomes.
4. **Statistical Integration** selects methods appropriate to the design and may include descriptive statistics, stratified comparisons, parametric or non-parametric tests, effect sizes, confidence intervals, multiplicity correction, correlations, sensitivity analyses, and robustness assessment.
5. **Communication-Performance Interpretation** integrates exposure, interaction, normalized indicators, effect magnitude, uncertainty, associations, robustness, institutional context, editorial context, and platform context without collapsing them into a universal score.
6. **Evidence-Informed Decision Support** translates bounded findings into editorial planning, format testing, monitoring, hypothesis generation, and the design of longitudinal, experimental, qualitative, or mixed-method studies.

## Analytical flow

The workflow begins with a prespecified communication question, estimand or descriptive target, and documented context. Each platform variable is then mapped to a communication process and unit of observation. Data-quality and comparability checks precede derivation of normalized indicators. Statistical methods are selected after considering the design and distribution rather than by a fixed CPIF test battery. Interpretation synthesizes dimensions, magnitude, uncertainty, associations, and robustness. Decisions are framed as context-bound options or hypotheses, with an explicit statement of what the evidence cannot establish.

The flow is iterative. Findings at Layer 4 may reveal incomparability or missing context and require a return to Layers 1–3. Decision support may generate a new research question and a prospective data-collection plan. Such iteration should be documented rather than hidden.

## Outputs

Minimum CPIF outputs include:

- a context and metric-definition record;
- a justified mapping of metrics to primary and, where relevant, secondary communication dimensions;
- a reproducible account of cleaning, missingness, denominators, and derived indicators;
- statistical results reporting magnitude and uncertainty where estimable;
- a multidimensional, context-sensitive interpretation;
- a limitations statement separating performance from effectiveness;
- an evidence-informed decision or future-study agenda; and
- preserved data, code, metadata, and documentation subject to privacy, ethical, legal, platform-term, and licensing constraints.

## Applications

CPIF can support evaluation of university outreach accounts, campaign comparisons, content-format studies, monitoring of curricular extension projects, and cross-platform research when definitions are harmonized cautiously. It can also structure mixed-method studies by identifying where qualitative inquiry or direct effectiveness measures are needed. CPIF is not restricted to Instagram, but it does not assume that nominally similar metrics across platforms are equivalent.

## Limitations

The framework is presently a proposed methodological structure. It has not undergone external validation, multicentre application, formal consensus development, or prospective assessment. Platform data may contain undocumented estimation procedures, changing definitions, unavailable denominators, algorithmic mediation, and selection biases. Observational platform data generally do not support causal claims. Normalization can improve interpretability only when the denominator represents the intended opportunity for response and is measured consistently. CPIF cannot recover unobserved audience characteristics or educational and societal outcomes from traces alone.

## First empirical demonstration

The initial application uses 187 Instagram publications from Ocean Culture and PsicoCampus, two curricular university extension projects. The analytical set includes views, reach, likes, comments, shares, saves, follows, total interactions, interactions per 100 accounts reached, and likes per 100 accounts reached. The reach-normalized indicators quantify recorded action events per 100 accounts reached; they are not percentages or proportions of users and may exceed 100 when accounts can generate multiple events. The study uses project-stratified and post-format comparisons, rank-biserial effect sizes, Holm correction, Spearman correlations, sensitivity analysis, and Open Science practices. This is an empirical demonstration and proof of concept, not definitive validation of CPIF or evidence that Instagram metrics measure communication effectiveness.

## Recommendations for future validation

Future work should include independent conceptual review; stakeholder and expert consensus on domains and reporting items; multicentre applications across institutions and communication goals; cross-platform studies with explicit measurement-invariance questions; prospective preregistration; assessment of inter-rater reliability for metric classification and interpretation; convergent and discriminant comparisons with direct audience measures; longitudinal evaluation of decision usefulness; and transparent revision of framework components. A validation matrix should prespecify each intended claim or use, the required evidence, methods, stakeholder groups, and criteria for interpretation. Validation should test specific claims and uses rather than seek a single decontextualized validity label.
