# CPIF Conceptual Framework

## Definition

The Communication Performance Interpretation Framework (CPIF) is a structured analytical workflow designed to support the quantitative interpretation of digital science communication metrics. It organises evaluation into an iterative sequence that links communication objectives, platform-generated evidence, statistical analysis, contextual interpretation, and decision support.

## Purpose

CPIF addresses a recurring problem in digital communication evaluation: platform metrics are often interpreted as direct indicators of communication effectiveness even though they represent distinct observable processes. Reach and views mainly describe exposure; likes, comments, shares, and saves describe different forms of observable interaction; follows describe audience growth. None of these metrics alone demonstrates learning, attitude change, behavioural change, or social impact.

## Scope

CPIF is appropriate for observational analyses of communication initiatives conducted through social media and related digital platforms. It can be applied to university science communication, public engagement, extension projects, institutional communication, and other evidence-informed communication programmes.

## What CPIF is not

CPIF is not:

- a predictive model;
- a causal model;
- a psychometric scale;
- a universal performance index;
- a substitute for audience research;
- proof of learning, persuasion, or behavioural change.

## Core contribution

The framework's contribution is methodological: it requires analysts to define the communication context before selecting metrics, classify metrics according to the process they represent, prepare data transparently, integrate appropriate statistical evidence, interpret findings within contextual and inferential limits, and translate results into proportionate decisions.
