# CPIF Interpretation Rules

## Exposure metrics

Reach, views, and impressions support statements about potential or recorded exposure. They do not, on their own, support claims about comprehension, learning, persuasion, trust, or behavioural change.

## Interaction metrics

Likes, comments, shares, and saves represent different observable actions and should not be collapsed without justification. Total interactions may be reported as a descriptive aggregate, but component metrics should remain available.

## Audience-growth metrics

New follows indicate account-level audience growth associated temporally with content exposure. They do not identify motivation or long-term engagement.

## Comparisons

A statistically significant comparison should be interpreted using:

- group medians or other appropriate summaries;
- effect size;
- sample sizes;
- multiplicity adjustment;
- contextual comparability;
- plausible platform or content explanations.

## Correlations

Correlations describe co-variation, not direction or causation. Very high correlations between views and reach may indicate closely related exposure processes rather than independent dimensions of performance.

## Format effects

Differences between carousels, static images, and reels should be described as associations between format and recorded metrics unless assignment to format was controlled experimentally.

## Project effects

Differences between projects may reflect theme, audience, account history, follower base, editorial choices, or unmeasured contextual factors. Avoid attributing them solely to project identity.

## Recommended language

Use:

- "was associated with";
- "showed higher recorded interaction";
- "the evidence indicates";
- "within the observed period";
- "after adjustment for multiple comparisons";
- "the magnitude of the difference was...".

Avoid:

- "caused";
- "proved effectiveness";
- "generated impact";
- "changed behaviour";
- "engaged the audience" when only passive or ambiguous metrics were measured.
