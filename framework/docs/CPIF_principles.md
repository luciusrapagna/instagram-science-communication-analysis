# CPIF Principles

1. **Context precedes metrics.** Metrics have no stable meaning outside the communication objective, audience, platform, and publication conditions.
2. **Metrics represent processes, not outcomes by default.** Exposure, interaction, and audience growth must be interpreted separately.
3. **No single metric defines communication performance.** Performance is multidimensional and question-dependent.
4. **Statistical significance is insufficient.** Effect sizes, distributions, uncertainty, multiplicity, and practical meaning must be considered.
5. **Observational data require restrained inference.** Associations and group differences do not establish causality.
6. **Normalisation must be transparent.** Every rate must report its numerator, denominator, scale, and rationale.
7. **Platform data are constructed evidence.** Definitions and recording rules may change and must be documented.
8. **Zero-valued metrics are meaningful but distribution-sensitive.** Sparse metrics require appropriate summaries and tests.
9. **Robustness should be examined.** Outlier sensitivity, stratification, and alternative specifications improve interpretive credibility.
10. **Decisions must be proportionate to evidence.** Recommendations should not exceed what the data can support.
11. **The workflow is iterative.** Later findings may require revision of earlier classifications or analytical choices.
12. **Reproducibility is part of interpretation.** Data dictionaries, scripts, versioning, and reporting decisions should be preserved.
