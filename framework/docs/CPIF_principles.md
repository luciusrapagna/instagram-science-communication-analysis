# CPIF principles

The Communication Performance Interpretation Framework is guided by five principles. These principles define the minimum conceptual commitments required for a CPIF-aligned analysis.

## Principle 1 — Communication performance is multidimensional

### Definition

Communication performance comprises distinct but related dimensions that cannot be adequately represented by a single platform metric.

### Rationale

Platform-recorded metrics describe different stages or processes of digital communication. Exposure indicates content distribution and visibility, whereas interaction indicates observable audience actions. Treating one metric as a complete representation of performance obscures these distinctions.

### Analytical implication

Analyses should examine multiple relevant dimensions separately and then integrate their interpretation. A composite indicator may be reported only when its components and construction are transparent, but it must not be treated as a universal performance score.

### Common error avoided

This principle prevents studies from treating a high number of likes, views, or any other isolated metric as evidence of globally superior communication performance.

### Generic example

A publication may achieve broad reach but generate few observable interactions, while another may reach fewer accounts but produce a higher interaction rate. These patterns represent different performance profiles.

### Application to the current study

In the initial Instagram application, views and reach represent exposure, whereas likes, comments, shares, saves, follows, and derived interaction indicators represent interaction. These dimensions are analysed separately before a contextualized interpretation is developed.
## Principle 2 — Platform metrics represent distinct communication processes

### Definition

Each platform-recorded metric must be interpreted according to the communication process, counting unit, and platform-specific definition it represents.

### Rationale

Metrics with different labels may describe exposure, interaction, audience growth, content consumption, or navigation behaviour. Even similarly named metrics may use different counting rules across platforms or over time.

### Analytical implication

Researchers should document the operational definition, unit, extraction date, and analytical role of each metric. Metrics should be grouped by communication process without assuming direct equivalence between platforms.

### Common error avoided

This principle prevents the indiscriminate use of the term engagement for all platform metrics and avoids treating views, accounts reached, impressions, likes, shares, and clicks as interchangeable observations.

### Generic example

Impressions may count repeated displays of content, whereas reach may estimate unique accounts exposed to it. A like records a different process: an observable action associated with the content.

### Application to the current study

In the initial Instagram application, views and reach are classified as exposure metrics. Likes, comments, shares, saves, and follows are retained as distinct recorded actions, while total interactions and reach-normalized indicators are documented as derived analytical variables.
## Principle 3 — Statistical interpretation should extend beyond descriptive reporting

### Definition

The interpretation of communication performance should combine descriptive evidence with statistical methods appropriate to the study design, data structure, and research questions.

### Rationale

Counts, percentages, medians, and distributions describe the observed sample but do not, by themselves, quantify the magnitude, uncertainty, or robustness of differences and associations. Statistical integration can provide additional evidence when its assumptions and limitations are explicit.

### Analytical implication

Researchers should select methods according to the study design, distribution of the data, sample structure, number of groups, dependence between observations, and analytical objectives. Where appropriate, analyses should report effect sizes, confidence intervals, multiplicity control, associations, and sensitivity or robustness assessments rather than relying only on p-values.

### Common error avoided

This principle prevents conclusions based exclusively on descriptive differences or statistical significance without considering effect magnitude, uncertainty, multiple comparisons, assumptions, and analytical stability.

### Generic example

Two content formats may have different median interaction rates, but interpretation should also consider group sizes, distributional overlap, effect magnitude, uncertainty, and whether the comparison remains stable under reasonable sensitivity analyses.

### Application to the current study

The initial Instagram application combines descriptive statistics with project and post-format comparisons, rank-biserial effect sizes, Holm correction, Spearman correlations, and sensitivity analysis. These procedures support contextual interpretation but do not establish causality or definitive validation of CPIF.
## Principle 4 — Communication performance is context-dependent

### Definition

Communication performance must be interpreted in relation to the institutional, editorial, temporal, audience, and platform conditions under which the metrics were recorded.

### Rationale

Platform metrics are shaped by factors beyond content characteristics, including communication objectives, audience composition, publication schedules, account history, platform algorithms, paid promotion, cumulative exposure time, and institutional resources. Consequently, the same metric value may have different meanings in different contexts.

### Analytical implication

Researchers should document the communication initiative, objectives, intended audience, institution, platform, observation period, editorial calendar, publication format, promotion strategy, and relevant changes in platform conditions. Comparisons should be stratified, adjusted, or explicitly qualified when contextual differences may affect interpretation.

### Common error avoided

This principle prevents decontextualized comparisons between projects, accounts, formats, periods, or platforms and avoids presenting one observed value as a universal benchmark.

### Generic example

A post published by an established account with paid promotion and a long observation period cannot be directly compared with an organically distributed post from a new account observed for only a few days without considering these differences.

### Application to the current study

The initial application examines two curricular university extension projects with potentially different audiences, editorial practices, content distributions, and communication contexts. Analyses therefore include project-stratified and post-format comparisons, while conclusions remain limited to the observed projects, period, platform, and study design.
## Principle 5 — Platform metrics are proxies of communication processes rather than direct measures of educational or societal impact

### Definition

Platform-recorded metrics provide indirect evidence about communication exposure and interaction, but they do not directly measure communication effectiveness.

### Rationale

Views, reach, likes, comments, shares, saves, follows, and clicks are behavioural traces produced within a platform environment. They do not independently demonstrate learning, knowledge acquisition, scientific literacy, trust in science, behavioural change, community participation, dialogue quality, or societal impact.

### Analytical implication

Researchers must distinguish communication performance from communication effectiveness. Claims about effectiveness require outcome-specific measurements and study designs, such as knowledge assessments, validated questionnaires, interviews, behavioural observations, longitudinal follow-up, experimental comparisons, or community-level indicators.

### Common error avoided

This principle prevents researchers from interpreting high exposure or interaction as direct evidence that audiences learned, changed attitudes, adopted behaviours, or experienced social benefits.

### Generic example

A widely shared science-communication post may have achieved substantial distribution and observable interaction, but the sharing count alone cannot establish whether users understood the content or changed their behaviour.

### Application to the current study

The initial Instagram application directly evaluates platform-recorded exposure and interaction. It does not measure learning, scientific literacy, trust, behavioural change, community participation, or societal impact. The findings must therefore be interpreted as communication-performance evidence rather than evidence of communication effectiveness.
